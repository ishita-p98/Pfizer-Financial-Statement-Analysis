# 10 — Reconciliation checks and methodological notes

This chapter is intentionally prominent because a recruiter may test the **consistency of inputs and definitions**, not just the quality of narrative interpretation.

## 1. Revenue figures differ across chapters
The report's revenue analysis gives **2024 revenue of $63.628bn** (pp. 18–20), while the profitability and ratio tables use **$61.513bn** (pp. 40, 50). The same issue appears in earlier years (e.g., 2022 revenue $101.175bn versus $100.330bn).

**Treatment:** Both sets are identified by their source section; they are not silently combined. Any final financial model must reconcile scope, restatements, reporting conventions and source filings.

## 2. Net income differs by accounting scope
The 2024 ratio chapter uses **$7.235bn** (p. 40), while the earnings-quality section uses **$8.05bn from continuing operations** (p. 59). These are not necessarily interchangeable. The appropriate numerator depends on whether the calculation uses income attributable to shareholders, consolidated net income or continuing operations.

**Treatment:** Label the earnings definition before calculating ROE, margin or NI/CFO.

## 3. Return on equity denominator
The report defines ROE using **average equity**, but its 2021 and 2022 table labels indicate year-end equity while later years use averages (p. 39). Mixed denominators reduce time-series comparability.

**Treatment:** Recompute consistently using average beginning/end equity when authorized annual statement values are available, and disclose any exceptions.

## 4. “Debt-to-equity” is actually total liabilities/equity
The ratio formula on p. 44 uses **total liabilities / shareholders' equity**. This differs from financial debt/equity and net debt/equity.

**Treatment:** Call the original measure **liabilities-to-equity** and use a separate interest-bearing debt measure for capital structure and credit analysis.

## 5. CFO + CFI is not standard free cash flow
The report interprets CFO relative to investing cash flow in its cash-flow chapter. Because investing activities include **acquisitions, investment securities and Haleon proceeds**, CFO + CFI cannot be labeled recurring free cash flow.

**Treatment:** Start with **CFO minus capital expenditure** as a simple proxy, then separately consider acquisitions, divestitures, financing and other valuation adjustments.

## 6. Adjustments need a full bridge
The report discusses removing inventory charges, restructuring costs, amortization and discrete tax items (pp. 36–38), and provides illustrative adjusted figures. However, a reproducible GAAP-to-adjusted bridge must establish each item, tax effect and consistency of the earnings basis.

**Treatment:** Do not present the illustrative adjusted net income as an independently verified calculation.

## 7. Distress and earnings-quality indicators are screens
Altman Z-score is not a calibrated default probability. NI/CFO above or below a threshold is not conclusive proof of accounting quality. Repeated EPS misses do not rule out discretionary accounting.

**Treatment:** Interpret these as **supporting indicators**, alongside disclosures, maturity profiles, audit matters and normalized cash generation.

## 8. Peer comparisons need consistent definitions
The report's peer tables and common-size appendix do not always present the same revenue totals or ratio denominators. The peer ranking should not be treated as audited cross-company standardization without a source-level reconciliation.

## 9. Date and investment scope
The report was submitted **4 December 2025**, primarily analyzing **FY2021–FY2024**. Its Moderate Buy conclusion is a historical classroom view, not a current recommendation.

## 10. What remains to verify
A full independent audit would require the underlying annual-report line items, source spreadsheets and any calculation workbooks used by the group. Those were not provided with this PDF. This repository therefore distinguishes **report-derived figures** from **new analytical cautions** and makes no claim of end-to-end numerical replication.

**Sources:** Original report pp. 18–20, 36–45, 50–63 and 64–67.
