# 07 — Earnings quality and accounting judgment

## The key question
How much of Pfizer's reported profit and apparent recovery reflects **recurring economics**, rather than accounting estimates, acquisition charges, pandemic-specific items or temporary cash-flow timing?

## Areas examined in the report
1. **Non-GAAP adjustments:** acquired intangible amortization, restructuring, impairments, acquisition-related costs and other specified items.
2. **Net income versus CFO:** whether reported profit is supported by operating cash generation.
3. **Receivables and allowances:** whether collections or credit-loss assumptions diverge from sales.
4. **Intangible impairments:** whether acquired technology and in-process R&D remain supportable.
5. **Analyst EPS comparisons:** whether reported earnings diverge from expectations.

## Earnings versus cash
The report gives 2024 net income from continuing operations of about **$8.05bn** and CFO of **$12.744bn**, producing NI/CFO of roughly **0.63**. CFO exceeding earnings is informative, but does **not prove** an absence of earnings management: depreciation, amortization, working capital and one-off payments can all influence the ratio.

## Acquired intangibles
The report discusses roughly **$18.9bn in in-process R&D assets** considered particularly sensitive to impairment. Recoverability depends on clinical, regulatory and commercial outcomes. Goodwill and technology valuations can be sensitive to revenue assumptions and discount rates.

## Non-GAAP skepticism
Adding back acquisition amortization may help compare cash earnings, but repeated acquisition spending can make those costs economically relevant. Similarly, restructuring costs may recur over several years. A strong analysis reconciles GAAP and non-GAAP measures and considers both.

## EPS surprise
The report's analyst comparison shows GAAP EPS below its stated consensus estimates in all four years. This does not, by itself, establish either manipulation or the absence of manipulation. Analyst estimates must be aligned to the same **GAAP/non-GAAP basis** and forecast date.

## Balanced conclusion
The submitted report assesses earnings-management risk as low. A more defensible reading is that the selected indicators **do not establish obvious manipulation**, while major estimation areas and earnings adjustments still warrant scrutiny. This is an analytical qualification, not an allegation.

**Report reference:** pp. 8–9, 36–38 and 59–63.
