# 06 — Profitability, liquidity and credit risk

## Why ratios require interpretation
A ratio is only as useful as its **numerator, denominator, accounting basis and peer comparability**. This is especially important when acquisitions, pandemic revenues and share repurchases distort financial statements.

## Profitability
The report's ratio chapter shows Pfizer ROE falling from about **32.8% in 2022** to **2.2% in 2023**, then recovering to **8.1% in 2024**. ROA similarly declined from **15.9%** to **0.9%**, recovering to **3.3%**. These figures reflect both earnings changes and a larger asset base.

ROE is not a pure operational-quality measure: debt financing, buybacks and a small equity denominator can inflate it.

## Liquidity
| Ratio | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current ratio | 2.31× | 1.58× | 1.48× |
| Quick ratio | 1.98× | 1.32× | 1.24× |

Both ratios decline after the pandemic peak but remain above 1× in the submitted calculations. This is reassuring **only in context**: cash accessibility, receivable quality, working capital timing and committed facilities also matter.

## Leverage: define the metric
The report labels **total liabilities / shareholders' equity** as “debt-to-equity.” For 2024, this is **1.41×**. Technically, this is a **liabilities-to-equity ratio**, not interest-bearing financial debt divided by equity. It includes operating liabilities and should not be equated directly with net debt or credit leverage.

## Interest coverage
The report's 2024 EBIT / interest expense measure is **5.6×**, versus **1.8× in 2023**. This suggests recovery in reported earnings coverage, but a credit assessment should also consider cash interest, maturities, refinancing costs and the stability of EBIT.

## Altman Z-score
The report presents a 2024 Z-score of **5.7** and interprets it as indicating low distress risk. Such a score is a **screening statistic**, not a calibrated probability of default. Applicability depends on the chosen Altman specification, industry, market-value inputs and data quality; it should not override ratings, cash-flow analysis or debt maturities.

## Debt and leases
The report cites approximately **$63.1bn of long-term debt at year-end 2024**, versus $69.5bn in 2023, reflecting the post-Seagen financing position. Lease obligations are discussed separately; lease-adjusted debt can help comparability, but lease and debt definitions must be explicit.
