# 08 — Peer benchmarking: Pfizer, Merck, Eli Lilly and AbbVie

## Why compare these firms?
The group selected three major innovative biopharmaceutical peers. Their business models share patent, pipeline, pricing and regulatory exposure, but their **product mixes, acquisition histories and accounting structures differ**.

## Interpreting the differences
- **Pfizer:** post-COVID earnings normalization and debt-financed portfolio renewal depress comparability with 2022.
- **Eli Lilly:** strong 2024 growth and a relatively small equity base contribute to high ROE; ROE alone does not establish the best risk-adjusted investment.
- **Merck:** relatively stable revenue and earnings profile in the report, though specific R&D and acquisition charges affect year-by-year comparisons.
- **AbbVie:** Humira's loss of exclusivity and acquisition-driven intangible assets influence margins and leverage.

## Important comparability issues
- **Accounting bases:** GAAP earnings versus adjusted earnings and classification of acquired R&D may differ.
- **Leverage definitions:** total liabilities/equity includes trade and operating obligations; it is not net debt/EBITDA.
- **ROE denominator effects:** buybacks and accumulated losses can reduce equity, inflating ROE.
- **Product-cycle timing:** Pfizer's COVID peak, AbbVie's patent cliff and Lilly's metabolic growth are not equivalent operating environments.
- **Scale:** absolute cash balances do not measure liquidity adequacy without obligations and financing access.

The peer comparison is strongest as a **diagnostic tool**, not a simple ranking.
