# 05 — Cash flow and capital allocation

## A three-statement narrative
The report describes a shift from extraordinary pandemic operating inflows to acquisition-led capital deployment and subsequent deleveraging. Distinguishing **operating, investing and financing cash flow** is essential because their economic meanings differ.

| USD bn | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cash from operations | 32.922 | 29.267 | 8.700 | 12.744 |
| Cash from investing | −22.596 | −15.783 | −32.278 | +2.652 |
| Cash from financing | −9.816 | −14.834 | +26.066 | −17.140 |

*Values as presented in the report's cash-flow chapter; these are historical amounts, not forecasts.*

## Operating cash flow
CFO fell sharply in 2023 as COVID-related demand and working capital normalized, then rose **46.5% in 2024**. A rebound from a depressed base does not by itself establish a sustainable run rate.

## Investing cash flow and Seagen
The **$32.278bn investing outflow in 2023** reflects the major Seagen acquisition. The 2024 **$2.652bn inflow** includes about **$7bn in proceeds from partial sales of Pfizer's Haleon stake**. A positive CFI here is not evidence that ongoing capital expenditure became cash-generative.

## Financing cash flow
The report links 2023's **$26.066bn financing inflow** to approximately **$30.8bn in long-term debt issuance**. In 2024, the **$17.140bn outflow** reflects repayments and distributions, including approximately **$11.226bn of net short-term borrowing repayments** and **$9.512bn of dividends**.

Debt-financed acquisition and subsequent repayment should be assessed together with the acquired asset's expected returns.

## CFO and capital expenditure
The report records CapEx of about **$2.909bn in 2024**, implying:
- **CFO / CapEx:** approximately **4.38×**.
- **CFO minus CapEx:** approximately **$9.835bn**.

The latter is a simple cash-flow proxy, **not** a complete valuation free-cash-flow-to-firm measure. It is only modestly above the report's **$9.512bn dividend payments**, before considering debt maturities, other cash demands and liquidity buffers. This is a more nuanced way to evaluate distribution headroom than CFO/CapEx alone.

## Common analytical traps
- CFO + CFI is **not** conventional free cash flow when CFI includes acquisitions, securities transactions and divestitures.
- Proceeds from Haleon stake sales are **nonrecurring capital recycling**, not recurring operating profitability.
- R&D is generally expensed under U.S. GAAP; cash investment in research is not captured by CapEx alone.
- A single year's cash balance is not a complete measure of liquidity.
