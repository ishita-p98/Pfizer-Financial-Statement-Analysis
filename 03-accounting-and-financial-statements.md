# 03 — Accounting information and common-size analysis

## Why accounting policy matters
Reported assets, margins and revenue reflect both business activity and accounting estimates. The report identifies several areas where judgment is especially consequential: U.S. rebate accruals, uncertain tax positions, product litigation and acquired intangible valuations.

## Audit matters and interpretation
The report's audit discussion highlights:
- **Rebates and variable consideration:** revenue is recorded net of estimated discounts, rebates, chargebacks and returns; timing differences and product-specific assumptions matter.
- **Tax uncertainties:** cross-jurisdiction interpretation and examination outcomes can change liabilities.
- **Litigation reserves:** loss recognition depends on probability and estimation.
- **Acquired intangible assets:** fair values depend on product cash-flow forecasts, discount rates, regulatory outcomes and market assumptions.

An audit opinion does not remove estimation risk. Critical audit matters highlight complexity, not necessarily misstatement.

## Balance sheet: acquired assets dominate
The common-size appendix reports for Pfizer in 2024:
- Goodwill: **32%** of assets.
- Other intangibles, net: **26%**.
- Combined: **58%**.

The report contrasts this with approximately 70% for AbbVie, 34% for Merck and 15% for Eli Lilly. These proportions reflect different acquisition histories and accounting recognition; they are **not direct measures of economic franchise value**.

## Income statement: margins normalize
Pfizer's 2024 common-size income statement in the report shows:
- Cost of sales: **28% of revenue**, versus 42% in 2023.
- SG&A: **23%**.
- R&D: **17%**.
- Net income attributable to Pfizer shareholders: about **13%**.

The 2023 outlier should be understood alongside COVID-product inventory charges and the changed sales mix. The report's separate profitability-ratio section uses a different revenue and net-income basis; see the [reconciliation note](10-reconciliation-and-methodological-notes.md).

## Analyst judgment
An analyst should ask whether changes reflect recurring pricing, product mix and efficiency—or nonrecurring write-downs, acquisition accounting and denominator effects. Common-size statements are useful for cross-company comparison but cannot, by themselves, normalize those differences.
